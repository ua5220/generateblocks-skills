The Problem

Your Traffic Is Not the Problem

You are getting visitors. SEO is working. Ads are running. But conversions? Flat.

Most businesses throw money at getting more traffic when the real problem is what happens after people land on the site. A 2% conversion rate means 98 out of every 100 visitors leave without taking action. That is not a traffic problem. That is a conversion problem.

I have audited hundreds of websites. The patterns are consistent:

  • Headlines that talk about features instead of outcomes

  • CTAs buried below the fold or lost in visual noise

  • Forms asking for 12 fields when 3 would do

  • Mobile experiences that are afterthoughts

  • Trust signals missing at decision points

  • Page speed killing conversions before they start

The math is simple: Doubling your traffic costs 2x. Doubling your conversion rate costs a fraction of that and compounds forever. A 1% to 2% conversion rate improvement on ₹10L monthly revenue is ₹10L extra. Every month.

FAQ

Common Questions

How much traffic do I need for CRO?

Minimum 10,000 monthly visitors for meaningful A/B tests. Below that, I focus on best-practice implementations rather than statistical testing.

How long until I see results?

Quick wins from the audit within 2-4 weeks. A/B tests need 2-4 weeks to reach significance. Real compounding happens at the 3-month mark.

Do you guarantee results?

I guarantee the process and quality of work. Specific conversion lifts depend on your baseline, traffic quality, and implementation. Most clients see 20-50% improvements.

What platforms do you work with?

WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, custom builds. Most A/B testing tools work across platforms. I recommend tools based on your specific needs.

Do I need to provide design/dev resources?

For Audit and Growth packages, I handle everything. For complex custom development, we may need your dev team or I can quote implementation separately.

What is your testing methodology?

95% statistical significance minimum. Bayesian or Frequentist depending on traffic. I never call tests early. If traffic is low, I run longer or use different methods.
